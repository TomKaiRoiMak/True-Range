1.0.0 - first release

- Rings on the ground showing how far monsters really reach, with the server's real numbers (weapons, mods and buffs included):
  - Red: step inside it (your middle) and its attack lands on you
  - Yellow: inside it, it starts attacking (shown when different from red)
  - Purple: its splash damage, around whoever it attacks
- Green ring: your own reach (a target whose edge touches it can be hit); faint green: where you start swinging
- Which monsters: the one under your mouse, plus the ones after you (or every one nearby: a setting)
- Key (R by default): show every ring near you on / off
- Player settings: which monsters, your own ring (off / with the key / in a fight / always), the key, the yellow ring
- Harmless critters (birds, butterflies, rabbits...) get no rings
- Bosses' own attacks, read from the game's code: Bearger's swipe (6 around a point 1 in front of it - only the front half hits) replaces its plain red ring; the Armored Bearger's butt slam (purple, behind it)
- Ground pounds (purple): every monster that uses the game's ground pound shows how far it reaches
- By default every monster near you and your own ring show all the time
- Deerclops (and Tropical Experience's ice Deerclops): its ice spikes reach 9 in front (a cone 35 degrees to each side) and 4 all round; the Crystal Deerclops' ice lance shows around whoever it's after
- The yellow ring always shows: inside it the monster stops and attacks, outside it walks after you (when it's the same as the red ring, only red shows)
- Attack warnings: while a boss winds up, the real shape it will hit is outlined with orange dots, turning with it - Bearger's swipes (a half circle in front), ground pounds, the Armored Bearger's butt slam, Deerclops' ice spikes (a narrow fan in front and a small circle around it), its laser (a strip), the Crystal Deerclops' ice lance (around where you stood)
- Attack warnings are drawn with solid lines (a thick outline, faint lines inside)
- Bosses' fan-shaped attacks (Deerclops' spikes, Bearger's swipe) show as their real fan all the time, not a circle
- Your body ring: a small ring around you (the part attacks have to reach) - orange while you stand where an attack reaches you, red when you're hit (setting "Your body ring")
- Lines and warnings lie on the ground under characters (the mod's own shapes), and warnings are filled in
- Before a monster attacks, its rings and shapes are faint; the warning while it winds up is bright
- Monsters' bodies: a faint ring shows where you bump into them
- Ground pounds (Bearger's, the Armored Bearger's butt slam) show their real waves: where each one reaches, a ring closing in as the moment comes, then the wave front rolling out on time and the part it has swept
- Warnings are red, their inside breathing in and out; rings and shapes shown before an attack are at 75% so they read but stay apart from a real warning
- Deerclops' ice spikes come out near to far: the warning shows that wave rolling out on time
- Bearger's next attack: its ground pound's purple ring fades back in as the 10-second cooldown runs out, then blinks (with the reach of each wave inside): that means its next attack will be the pound, not a swipe - read from how its stategraph picks
- The warning no longer pulses
- When Bearger's pound is ready and what it's after comes into its reach, the pound's red warning shows right away - before it starts the move
- Attack warnings for every monster: the mod comes with the attack moves of 760+ monsters (the game's, Uncompromising Mode's and Tropical Experience's), read from their code - which animation starts each attack and on which frame it lands. When one starts, its reach fills in red towards the moment it hits
- The server learns: each time a monster's attack lands, it notes how far and how wide (in front of it) it reached and when; after a few hits the warning takes that shape. What it learns is kept between games and sent to players only for the monsters they meet, and only when something new was learned
- Charges, beams and what comes next, learned by watching bosses and big monsters: how far a move carries it (a charge's path is drawn from where it set off to where it will stop, as wide as its hits reach), hits that always land in a straight line ahead (a beam), and which move usually follows which - during a wind-up, the charge or beam it leads into already shows along where it faces
- A charge's path stays where the charge started (it no longer slides along with the monster when its animation loops); the part it has already passed disappears, and the stop point stays put
- A charge's path is as wide as its hits actually reach to its sides (it was too wide), and reaches as far past the stop point as its hits land in front of it
- Charge paths and beams have properly round ends (the filled band stuck out past them in square corners)
- How far a move carries a monster now averages every time it's used, not just the times it moved: a charge's wind-down that slides now and then is no longer taken for another charge (what was learned about travel before is relearned)
- The next charge or beam only shows when it follows that move at least half the time - and it shows already while the previous charge winds down, for monsters that charge again and again
- What comes after a move is learned apart for when the monster turned during it (took aim) and when it held still: a charge that winds down into another shows the next one once it turns to aim, and the last charge of a run (it holds still) no longer warns of one more
- A learned attack shape is never smaller than the hit range the game checks (it only knew how far hits had happened to land, so it could shrink far below the real reach)
- Bosses' attacks measured exactly, without anyone getting hit: while a boss's move is still being learned, its attack is shown points that aren't really there (probes) scattered over the area it looks at; its own code decides which it hits, and those are only noted (no damage, nothing happens in the world). Which points were hit and which weren't gives the real shape - its reach, its width, blind spots, a circle in front of it - after the first time it's used, sharpened over 5 rounds, then probing stops for good. Attack code that can't take probes is left alone after one try
- Probes also measure damage a move sets off a moment later (Deerclops' ice spikes), and a shape can be in two parts: a circle all round the boss plus a fan (Deerclops: spikes close by all round, and a cone far ahead)
- Leaps told apart from charges: a move that only hits once it has come most of the way is a leap - only where it lands is drawn, not the whole way there; and a leap that lands where its target stood (the Nightmare Werepig's slam) is drawn on its target
- Attacks that only ever hit what the monster is after (Klaus' swipes and chomp) are told apart from area attacks: drawn all round it out to its reach (no blind spot - it turns to whoever it's after), red and warning only if you're the one it's after; just outlined when it's after someone else
- Leaps that aim again on the way (Klaus' chomp) are recognised: whether a move is a leap is judged against each leap's own length, and a leap counts as going for its target when it ends up by it - drawn on its target as it moves
- Things bosses set off are measured too - crystals, spikes, lasers, anything that hurts from its own code a moment after the boss's move - each around itself; players near the boss see them outlined where they lie, and turn orange inside one
- Attacks that land in the same pattern every time but fit no fan (the Celestial Champion's lasers going three ways) are drawn as a map of where they really hit
- Moves that chase (the Celestial Champion's spin) are drawn as a path from the boss to whoever it's after, following them

- Whether a move is a leap is also judged from where the monster is when its damage comes, so dodging every time no longer hides it
- Probes no longer skip attacks that also smash trees and rocks on the way (the Celestial Champion's spin and spike walls)
